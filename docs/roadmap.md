## What has not been verified

- **HexForge by ear (0.50.0).** Its presets were measured, not listened to; CPU use with 8 unison voices of six operators at
  twice the sample rate on a real machine, and the VST3 in other programs, were not tried.

- **SunBurst by ear and in other hosts (0.48.0).** Its presets were measured, not listened to; CPU use with many unison voices
  on a real machine, the VST3 in other programs (Bitwig, Reaper, Ardour), the Standalone with a real audio interface and MIDI
  keyboard, and HiDPI scaling were not tried. Effect plugins are not yet told the tempo (with a play head set, a VST3 effect's
  parameter set from the host did not reach its saved state in the tests).

- **Count-in, loop takes and punch-out with a real audio interface (0.47.0).** The count-in's timing was measured in the Player
  only (the engine's count-in needs a running device); recording with a loop region, choosing takes by ear and punch-out were not
  tried; automation of the sends, aux buses and VCA groups was measured, not heard.

- **Tempo changes and time signatures by ear (0.46.0).** The clicks and note times were measured, not heard; playing across a
  tempo change with plugins, recording across one, and audio clips after one (they do not follow it) were not tried; the FL
  Studio importer still reads one tempo and 4/4.

- **The sidechain and the export options by ear (0.45.0).** The ducking was measured, not heard; the 16-bit dither was checked
  numerically; the tags were read back by JUCE only (not by a music player); 88.2 / 96 kHz exports of a project with plugins
  were not tried.

- **Many playlist tracks (0.44.0).** Scrolling was tried with 9 tracks; 128 tracks, dragging clips between tracks while scrolled,
  and the desktop no longer offering to open old `.slsproj` files after an update of the file type were not tried.

- **Crash reports on the real GitHub (0.43.0).** Issues were created only on a local server standing in for the API, with a test
  token: no issue was opened in the real repository, and a real fine-grained token was not tried. The browser path (no token) was
  not opened in a browser here. The repository has no release yet, so the real update check has only answered "no release".
  Crashes on the audio thread or inside a plugin were not provoked (the handler is process-wide, but only the main thread has the
  alternate stack for a stack overflow).

- **Moving pattern clips by ear (0.42.0).** Placement off the grid and the length buttons were checked by positions and note
  times, not listened to; an FL Studio export of a split pattern clip starts it at its pattern's start (FL Studio's own item offset
  is not written yet).

- **Stems on real music (0.39.0).** Measured on a generated mix only: how much bleed and how many artefacts (a "watery" Other)
  real songs give was not heard.
- **The pitch editor on real voices (0.38.0).** Tested on generated tones with harmonics and vibrato: breathy or noisy singing,
  consonants and slides between notes were not tried, nor how the shifted notes sound.
- **How the Slicer sounds (0.37.0).** Slices, cutting and the score are measured, not heard: whether the 5 ms fade of a cut
  slice clicks on real loops, and how *Slice by sound* finds hits on dense material, was not judged by ear.
- **Multitrack recording with a real interface (0.36.0).** Here the inputs were silent and the clock was not real: whether
  several real inputs line up to the sample, and how a slow disk copes with eight takes at once (each has a 3-second buffer),
  was not tried.
- **Exported .flp files in FL Studio (0.34.0).** FL Studio is not available here: the files follow FL Studio 20.8's layout and are
  read correctly by an independent reader, but whether FL Studio opens them without complaint has not been seen.
- **The typing keyboard's timing (0.32.0).** In the virtual display the audio device has no real clock (the song runs several
  times too fast), so notes recorded from the keyboard came out too long there; the key presses and releases themselves were
  seen to arrive at once. How it feels on a real machine was not tried.
- **How the Loop Starter's loops sound (0.30.0).** They are checked for key, rhythm and level, not heard: whether the grooves feel
  right and the melodies are pleasant is for a listener to say.
- **Many tracks on a real machine (0.29.0).** Measured here in a virtual display, where the app's audio thread busy-waits on a
  dummy device (about one core with 8 tracks or 500 alike): the cost of 500 tracks on a real sound card was not measured.
  Each edit records an undo step by writing the whole project out: about 3.5 ms with 300 tracks here.
- **The score log with a real MIDI keyboard (0.28.0).** The log itself is tested, but no MIDI keyboard could be connected here: the
  way from the keyboard to the log (stamping on the audio thread, the queue, putting the notes into the pattern) was not run.
- **The Pitch Corrector by ear (0.26.0).** Tested on synthetic tones (sines, harmonics, vibrato, glides, noise), not on a real
  voice: how natural it sounds on singing, and how it copes with breathy or rough voices, was not heard. It does not keep the
  formants (Pitcher has an option for that) and cannot take its target notes from MIDI yet.
- **The Time Shaper by ear (0.25.0).** The patterns are exact to the sample (tested), but how the presets feel in music was not heard.
  Only four Time Shapers per project get a recording (memory); a fifth only shapes the volume (the effects panel says so since 0.41.0).
- **How the additive and physical sounds sound (0.24.0).** Measured only (pitch, spectrum, decay): the 14 new sounds were set by
  numbers and were not listened to. The string's tuning is exact to 5 cents; its timbre and the body's resonances were not judged.
- **The piano roll's tools by hand (0.23.0).** Rubber-band selection, copy / paste at the mouse, duplicate, drawing velocities in the
  lane, the chord stamp and every Tools item were written and the theory under them is tested, but only some were clicked in the
  interface (above); whether the generated progressions and basslines *sound* good was not heard.
- **How the built-in sounds sound (0.22.0).** Nothing was heard. The piano, bass and acoustic kits are recordings changed only as
  `tools/sounds` describes (cut, levelled, mixed to stereo, Ogg Vorbis at quality 3 / 4); the mixdown of the drum microphones follows the
  kits' own SFZ mapping but was not judged by ear. The electronic and percussion kits are made by code and were only measured (pitch,
  brightness, level): whether they sound like good drums is not known. The demo was not listened to.
- **Memory of the sampled instruments.** The grand piano takes about 200 MB once decoded and the electric bass about 80 MB (mono
  recordings are stored as two equal channels); the demo uses both. Samples are not streamed from disk.
- **How the new effects sound (0.21.0).** Only measured. The presets were set by numbers, the reverb types and the tape delay by their
  structure, not by ear; the Maximizer was not compared with a commercial limiter, and true peaks between samples are not measured by it.
- **How the FM synth sounds (0.19.0).** Only measured (pitch, brightness, envelopes, bounds); the factory FM sounds were set by numbers.
- **How the subtractive synth sounds (0.18.0).** Pitch, filter, envelopes and LFO were measured on single notes; the factory sounds were
  shaped by numbers (levels, cutoffs, times), not by ear. Aliasing at very high notes with the square wave's pulse-width modulation, the
  resonance at its maximum and the CPU load of 32 subtractive voices on a real computer were not judged.
- **Plugins with real, commercial plugins (0.17.0).** Only the two small test plugins (made with JUCE, like most plugins) and the LADSPA
  example plugins were loaded. Plugins with their own graphical editors, plugins made with other frameworks (DPF, iPlug2, native LV2 such as
  the Calf or x42 sets), plugins with several outputs or side-chain inputs, heavy plugins at small buffer sizes, and how they behave when the
  audio device changes while they play were not tried. Nothing was heard.
- **How the sampler sounds (0.16.0).** Envelope, loops, filter and zones were measured on sine tones only. The loop of a real sample can click where its
  ends do not meet (there is no crossfade); the filter's resonance at high settings, and how SoundFont presets compare with other SoundFont players
  (attenuation uses the EMU / FluidSynth 0.4 factor; modulators, vibrato and the file's own filter are ignored), were not judged by ear. Only one
  real SoundFont (a 519 KB subset) was loaded; large ones (hundreds of MB) were not timed and block the window while they load. Dragging a `.sf2` from
  the browser onto a channel and the sampler window's sliders while playing were not tried.

- **0.15.0 by ear and on real hardware.** Jumping with the ruler and the loop point were measured on synthetic tones and watched in a virtual display whose silent device runs
  faster than real time; whether the jump clicks on real material, and how the 20 ms release at a seek sounds, was not judged by ear. *Delete channel*, *Clone channel* and the
  *Export project with samples* dialog were not clicked through in the interface (their data part is covered by tests); the confirmation dialog of *Delete channel* was not seen.
  The undo history copies the whole project at every edit: on very large projects (many long patterns) this was not timed. The CI workflow
  (`.github/workflows/build.yml`) ran once on GitHub for 0.15.0 (Fedora 41 container: build and all tests passed); it builds without
  `FETCHCONTENT_SOURCE_DIR_JUCE`, so it also checks the JUCE download.

- **How the drum machine sounds and plays on real hardware.** Layers, choke groups, levels and swing were checked on synthetic tones and by measuring rendered audio; the pads were clicked in a virtual display with a silent device,
  never heard on speakers, and never played from a real pad controller or MIDI keyboard. The 20 ms choke fade and the 0.5 ms attack were not judged by ear. Saving kits was tested on a local disk only
  (no read-only folders, no huge sounds). Loading a big kit blocks the window for as long as the files take to decode.

- **How the sample editor sounds and feels on real material.** Noise reduction (spectral gating) and hit detection were measured on synthetic signals only:
  the watery artefacts on real noise, and the hits found (or missed) in real drums, voices and loops, were not judged by ear. The preview and the spectrum
  were run against a silent device, never with real speakers. Large files (minutes of audio) were not timed in the editor; each edit copies the whole sample
  and the undo history is limited to 40 steps or a memory budget.

- **How stretched audio sounds on real music.** Length, pitch and timing were measured on synthetic tones only; the quality on drums, voices and full mixes
  (smearing of attacks, a metallic tone at 4x or an octave and more) was not judged by ear. There is no separate transient mode and no tempo detection:
  the source tempo has to be typed. The processing blocks the window for the few seconds it takes.

- **How automation, sends, buses and VCA groups sound on real hardware.** Everything was measured in rendered audio and seen in a virtual display;
  nothing was heard. Automation is evaluated once per 64 samples, so a very steep clip (a hold step on a loud signal) can click: not listened to.
  The on-screen controls do not follow automation while the song plays. **The meters were not compared with a hardware or commercial loudness meter**,
  only with the published reference signals; the true peak is a 4x estimate. They read the program's output, not an exported file.
- **How legato and slide sound on real hardware** (measured only: pitch and level in rendered audio) and **MIDI import on real-world files**: only
  generated files were used, not files from sequencers; the handling of tempo changes, drums and unusual files is as described in the guide.

- **How the Cyber-Hub sounds, the new demo and the start-up sound actually sound.** Nothing could be heard here: the start-up
  player and the demo were checked by unit tests (rate, length, block independence, levels) and by meters on a silent device.
  Whether the start-up sound plays at a comfortable loudness on a real device, and whether the demo's drums sit well together,
  is not known. **MIDI note recording from a real keyboard** (timing against the output latency, loop wrap by ear) was only
  tested with stamped events in unit tests.

- **Sound of the new features on real hardware.** Panels, patterns, the playlist, Song mode, pitch, swing, track
  routing and the effects were not heard through an audio device (the test environment had none); they are covered
  by offline-renderer tests and by meters in a virtual display. How the effects *sound* (EQ curve taste, reverb
  quality, compressor behaviour) has not been judged by ears. The playhead moving across the playlist during
  playback has not been seen.
- **How live notes sound on real hardware** (attack click, latency between click and sound, behaviour of a note held
  across a device restart). The virtual display's silent ALSA device runs faster than real time, so the 20-second limit
  ended a held note after about a second there; with the limit raised for the check the meter stayed lit for as long as
  the button was held.
- **The AppImage on a real Nobara machine.** It was built and run only on the build host (Ubuntu 24.04) in a virtual
  display. Not checked: FUSE mounting (the build host has no FUSE, so only `--appimage-extract-and-run` was used),
  the real audio device, the desktop's own file-type association for `.slp` / `.slsproj`, and the menu entry (AppImages do not
  create one without a helper such as AppImageLauncher).
- **Saving and opening on real data.** Only projects made in the tests and in the virtual display were used. The
  file chooser is JUCE's own, not the KDE dialog. Large projects were not timed, and the Save / Save as flow was not
  tried with a read-only folder or a full disk (a failed write is covered by a test that points the target at an
  impossible place and checks that the old file is untouched).
- **That the new default buffer and Auto stop the crackling on real hardware.** The crackling was reported on a real
  machine at a 512-sample buffer; here only a silent device was available, which never drops blocks, so Auto's
  detection of real dropouts (the audio system's count, ALSA/JACK) and the effect of the extra load were not seen
  with a computer that actually struggles. If 1024 is not enough, try a bigger size and tell us what **Audio...** shows
  (load, busiest block, dropped blocks). Whether PipeWire's ALSA layer reports every dropout is also unchecked; Auto
  says so when the count is unavailable.
- **How the master effects sound.** Only measured (levels, tails, bypass) and seen, not listened to on real hardware. The master chain was not checked in an exported WAV by ear.
- **Real input hardware.** No microphone, audio interface or MIDI keyboard was available: the input level, a take
  from a real device, the monitoring, MIDI notes from a real keyboard (and plugging one in while the program runs) and
  the device lists in the Audio dialog for real hardware were not tried. The MIDI path was checked only through the unit
  tests of the note handling.
- **Take alignment.** A take is shifted by the round-trip latency the device reports. Whether that lines up with the
  backing track on real hardware (some drivers report latency wrongly) was not measured. The virtual audio device runs
  faster than real time, so the Stop button during a take was not exercised with a real clock either (the automatic stop
  at the end of the playlist uses the same code).
- **ffmpeg on the user's machine.** AAC export and import, MP3 export and the sound of videos use `ffmpeg`; here it
  was ffmpeg 7 on the build host. MP3 import and every other format are decoded by the program itself. Other video
  containers than MP4 were not tried, nor videos with several audio tracks (the first one is used).
- **Dropping from the real file managers** (Dolphin, Nautilus) under Wayland: only the X11 protocol was simulated.
- **How the metronome sounds** on real hardware (the click is a 45 ms sine burst; nobody has listened to it).
- **Typing in the "Save as preset" name box** was not tested (the virtual display had no window manager, so dialogs
  do not receive keyboard focus); saving and loading presets is covered by the tests.
- **Memory of the effects**: about 22 MB at 48 kHz (8 tracks + master + 2 aux buses, 4 slots each), more at higher sample rates; not measured
  on a real device.
- **Latency.** The program shows what the device reports; it has never been measured.
- **The installed menu entry on KDE** (the file is installed and well-formed, but it was not seen in a real menu).
- **Compiler warnings on GCC 16** in `tests/test_core.cpp` (silenced with pragmas written against a message from
  GCC 16, tested here only with GCC 13).
- **Other distributions** than Nobara/Fedora.
- **Wayland-specific behaviour** (JUCE runs through XWayland).

## Proposed order of work

The feature blocks of [feature-plan.md](feature-plan.md) are done (0.11.0 - 0.41.0), and the lists of what was missing have been
worked through since (0.42.0 - 0.47.0). What comes next depends most on tests on real machines (see *What has not been verified*):

1. **Real-machine checks** on Nobara: audio interfaces, MIDI keyboards, plugins, the AppImage, crash reports with a token.
2. **Tempo and clips**: audio clips that follow tempo changes, tempo ramps, time-signature changes during the song.
3. **Automation**: the pitch, swing and master volume; effects on the aux buses; effect-panel sliders that move with it.
4. **Plugins**: presets, a sandbox process, side-chain inputs.
5. **Packaging**: Flatpak or RPM, and GitHub releases with the AppImage. The license is the AGPLv3 since 0.34.0 (see below).
